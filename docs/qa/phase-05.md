# Phase 5 — useful initiative and coordinated speech

13 October 2026. Local functional verification passed; live providers, physical acoustics and the startup performance target remain open.

## Implemented

The patient can request private unresolved-question cues, approve a shared public contribution, invite Vene under an agreed standing mode, or explicitly request sourced closeout. All present participants must separately agree to the speaking mode. Other addressees and quiet rooms do not trigger speech. Candidates have an eight-item bound, 90-second expiry, separate issuance digests, priority ranking, ten-second cooldown and current scope/version checks. Withdrawal followed by agreement does not resurrect an old approval.

The disclosed LiveKit participant publishes one public audio track. LiveKit AgentSession/SpeechHandle own playback and interruption, without RoomIO input, recording, automatic model turns or public transcript publication. Private cues are absent from guest REST responses and DOM. A shared question/step and all its sources must be visible to every role present. Clinical confirmation, patient authorization, required order and external completion remain separate.

Public output uses an initial authoritative database check, a dedicated commit-notification listener and periodic authoritative rechecks. Permission, scope, mode, pause and accepted human-interruption changes clear the small RTC output queue. Losing the listener fails closed. Notifications contain only a visit UUID and a reason. The listener runs in an owned thread because Windows' Proactor loop is needed for the offline synthesis subprocess; no extra transcript channel exists. Reader scope locks still serialize payload assembly with revocation; media permission snapshots last for one transaction only.

Human microphone admission is now independent of the voice worker's RTC handshake. As soon as signaling registers the browser identity, an authenticated, CSRF-protected endpoint verifies that exact participant with LiveKit and grants microphone-only publication under the visit lock. Verification runs alongside media connection establishment rather than waiting for a listen-only peer connection to finish. The administrative operation has a three-second overall deadline. Other sessions, expired/retired generations, absent participants and provider failure cannot obtain permission. This grants no processing consent and never reports the assistant ready just because a human joined. Initial signaling retries remain owned by the SDK's bounded connect attempt; rejoin cleanup applies to an already joined call. An assistant connection failure cannot replace a room where other verified humans are still present.

The fixture voice uses installed Windows System.Speech to synthesize original text in memory, reusing one owned engine between requests. A two-request primitive probe measured 1,076 ms cold and 56 ms warm, with identical 3.35-second generated duration; this is not an end-to-end percentile. The explicit live adapter uses livekit-plugins-cartesia 1.8.6 and sonic-3-2026-01-12. No failing provider becomes fixture success. A speech failure leaves the human conversation available. Restart cancels abandoned playback rather than replaying words whose delivery is unknown. Completed receipts retain full wording; interrupted receipts do not. Publisher playout and decoded receiver activity do not prove listener hearing.

Read-only scope polling, transient frame counters/activity and unchanged heartbeat/task counts use transaction-local asynchronous commits. The database default was verified as `on`; permissions, memberships, approvals, transcripts, changed participant/track authority, acknowledged stops and final playout receipts retain durable commits. Recent diagnostic counters may be lost on a hard crash. They are not clinical records or proof of hearing. The initial locked snapshot still runs for each permission check; no cross-frame authorization cache was introduced.

## Browser and visual evidence

`phase-05-browser-report.json`, `phase-05-private-cue.png` and `phase-05-paused-mobile.png` record the focused speech run. Desktop/private-cue and mobile/paused captures were inspected. The 390-pixel viewport had no horizontal overflow. Product status distinguishes listening, waiting, generation, speaking and pause, and shows publisher receipts without claiming hearing.

The final working-preview review used the actual fictional launcher and exercised private help after restart. `phase-05-preview-review.json` records no browser errors, no mobile overflow and preservation of the saved visit version. Final desktop/mobile captures were inspected; no room, physical device or provider call was used by this review. The isolated Chromium speech scenarios supply the real media evidence separately.

`phase-05-latency.json` contains all twenty unchanged samples and the measurement conditions. Two isolated Chromium instances used actual local LiveKit and the offline voice. The tone input's echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain control were disabled for repeatability; Chromium's loopback interface flag was enabled. The input buffer was 160 ms and publisher queue 80 ms. The room and fixed utterance remained unchanged across trials, with a 0.7-second quiet gap and test-only 0.2-second repeat cooldown. The measured input-to-audio interval includes the operator's private-cue request/approval gap; recognition delay is null, and the first trial includes cold output initialization. Failed/incomplete runs were retained as `phase-05-latency-before-tuning.json` and `phase-05-latency-before-warm-voice.json`; no percentile was claimed from them.

Earlier attempts failed and were not counted as passing evidence: the bot's JWT used an RPC enum instead of the VideoGrants string source; per-frame synchronous database checks stretched speech far beyond its audio duration; a waiting joined read could retain a prior call generation; and an immediate cue assertion raced its asynchronous refresh. One full suite correctly blocked speech because the earlier media test had silenced output. A later guest could replace a room generation after an assistant connection failure; this was corrected to preserve existing human conversations. Cold listen-only media negotiation and forced relay-only local connections also failed in some runs. The relay-only change was reverted; normal ICE is retained and controlled Chromium media tests enable the documented loopback interface. An early timing run completed twenty interruptions but hit its overall harness deadline before saving the full report; later incomplete timing data were retained without claiming percentiles. The final run records every sample and has a larger whole-experiment deadline without changing latency values. Processing agreement waits for the clinician's actual current published track. The final full regression passed after these repairs.

## Open gates

The local startup p95 missed the initial roughly two-second target. Live Deepgram recognition, real-model free-form extraction, live Cartesia quality/latency, physical microphone/speaker acoustics and end-to-end recognition delay remain unverified. Original microphone tone fixtures exercise transport and accepted-frame interruption, not clinical speech recognition. The twenty-trial local measurement does not establish production capacity or a clinical timing standard. No deployment, real patient data, healthcare calls or founder messaging occurred.
